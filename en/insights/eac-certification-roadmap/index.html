EAC certification: a practical roadmap for exporters to the Eurasian Economic Union

One conformity mark opens five markets, but the process rewards preparation. Here is how to plan certification without delaying your launch.

Elena Sokolova

One mark, five markets

Products covered by the Eurasian Economic Union's technical regulations must carry the EAC mark before they can be sold in Russia, Kazakhstan, Belarus, Armenia or Kyrgyzstan. The good news is that a single certificate or declaration of conformity is valid in all five countries.

Start with classification

The first step is to identify which technical regulations apply to your product: machinery safety, low-voltage equipment, electromagnetic compatibility and food-contact materials are common combinations. The applicable regulations determine whether you need a certificate issued by an accredited body or a declaration of conformity registered by the applicant.

Appoint a local applicant

Certificates and declarations are issued to an applicant registered in an EAEU member state — typically your subsidiary, your importer or an authorised representative. Choose this partner carefully: they carry legal responsibility for the documents and often keep control of them if the relationship ends.

Plan testing and labelling early

Testing must be carried out by laboratories accredited within the Union, and technical documentation and labelling must be available in Russian. For most industrial products, a well-prepared file can move from application to approval in a few weeks; incomplete technical documentation is the most common cause of delay. Above all, first confirm that the product and your counterparties are not restricted by the sanctions or export-control rules that apply to your company.
